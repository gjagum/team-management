OmniTask Philippines · Time management system

One place for hours, leave and overtime

This system records when contractors work, lets them ask for leave and overtime, and routes each request to the right person for approval. At the end of a pay period it produces a timesheet PDF that shows regular hours, approved overtime and what they are worth.

How a pay period moves

From a Slack message to a timesheet

Contractor

Clocks in and out

Posts "good morning" to start the day and "good evening" to end it in the attendance Slack channel.

Contractor

Asks for time

Requests leave or logs overtime in the web app, with dates, hours and a reason.

Team leader / Manager

Approves

Reviews the request, approves or rejects it, and can add a note. An alternate approver covers absences.

System

Totals the period

Combines scheduled hours with approved overtime at the overtime rate and produces the timesheet PDF.

What it means for you

Pick your role

You use the system to prove the hours you worked and to ask for time off or extra hours. Your login is labelled Employee in the app. That is the contractor role.

Every working day

  • Type good morning in the attendance channel when you start. Only your first one of the day counts.
  • Type good evening when you finish. Your last one of the day counts.
  • Check My schedule for your shift times and break.

When you need something

  • Leave: choose a type (vacation, sick, emergency…), dates and a reason. File it at least 3 days ahead unless it is urgent.
  • Overtime: log the date, start and end time and what you worked on, then submit it for approval.
  • Cancel a leave request yourself while it is still pending.

What you can see

  • Your leave balance. It grows through the year as you work (15 paid days a year by default).
  • The status of each request: pending, approved, rejected or cancelled.
  • Your own timesheet, schedule and documents.

Good to know

  • If a leave request needs more days than you have left, the extra days are marked unpaid automatically.
  • Overtime only counts toward pay once it is approved.
  • You cannot see other contractors' records.

Forgot to post in Slack, or posted in the wrong place? Tell your team leader the same day so the record can be fixed before the timesheet is generated.

You keep the team's time records accurate and make sure requests don't sit waiting. The system has two approver roles: Team Leader approves for their own team, and Manager approves across teams and sees reports.

Every working day

  • Open the dashboard and clear pending requests for leave and overtime.
  • Add a short note when you reject something so the contractor knows why.
  • Look for missing clock-ins or clock-outs and follow up the same day.

Every week

  • Check the coming week's schedules: shifts, days off and approved leave.
  • Keep overtime under the monthly cap (40 hours per contractor by default).

Each pay period

  • Generate or review timesheet PDFs for your people.
  • Check regular hours, overtime hours and the total before they go to payroll.
  • Export reports when finance or leadership asks.

What you can and can't do

  • You can view contractor profiles, teams, documents and onboarding progress.
  • You can't create users, change pay rates or edit company settings. Ask the administrator.
  • Going on leave yourself? Make sure your team's alternate approver is set.

Every approval is recorded with your name and the time. Approve only what you have checked.

You own the system: who can log in, what each role is allowed to do, and the company rules it applies (leave allowance, overtime rates, shift defaults). Contractors and managers depend on your setup being right on day one.

You set up

  • User accounts, contractor profiles, hire dates and hourly rates.
  • Teams, team leaders and alternate approvers.
  • Company settings: leave, overtime and schedule rules.
  • The Slack connection that records clock-ins.

You keep running

  • Joiners and leavers: create, update and deactivate accounts.
  • The permission matrix on the RBAC page.
  • The audit log, backups, document expiry and the yearly leave reset.

For the administrator

Known gaps to plan around

These come from the current code. Until they are fixed, the checklist above includes a workaround for each.

Deactivating a user doesn't log them out

A deactivated account keeps working until its session expires, up to 24 hours. Change the password as well when someone leaves on bad terms.

Slack messages aren't verified

The clock-in address doesn't check that a message really came from Slack. Keep the URL private and review odd time logs.

Clock-in date uses the server clock

On Cloudflare the server runs on UTC, 8 hours behind Manila. A "good morning" before 8:00 AM Manila time may land on the previous day. Test this before launch.

Leave balance can start at the wrong number

If a contractor files leave before they ever open their balance, the system uses 20 days instead of your setting and can show an invalid total. Have everyone open their balance once before filing leave.

Leave balance is calculated once a year

The year's balance is fixed the first time it is created and doesn't grow afterwards. Adjust balances by hand if your policy is earn-as-you-go.

Timesheet hours come from the schedule

Regular hours on the PDF are the scheduled shift minus break, not the Slack clock times. Schedules must be correct for pay to be correct.
